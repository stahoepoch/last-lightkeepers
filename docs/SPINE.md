# The spine — one session, one week, thirty hours

Status: **proposed**, 2026-10-26. Chris's shape, written out. Nothing here sets
a number; every figure is a placeholder marked `?` for him to set.

The test this page has to pass: *why does someone play their tenth run, and
their hundredth?* If a section below does not answer that, it is not done.

## First session — about 30 minutes

**You arrive at a hall that is almost entirely dark.** One room is lit: the
side room, because that is where the order actually met for years while the
main hall stood unfinished and unpaid for. The premise already carries this;
the opening should simply be true to it. Everything else you can see into and
not enter.

1. **The side room, 60 seconds.** Your lantern, the departure board, and the
   kitchen counter you will hand things in at. Nothing else is open, so nothing
   else needs explaining.
2. **The board asks one question:** alone, or with friends. Both are first
   class. Solo must never be the tutorial.
3. **One short run, the dock route.** ~4–6 minutes. It teaches the four rules
   by making you use them, in this order: lantern off and you can only take
   what is in plain sight; lantern on and the caches appear — and the meter
   starts climbing; taking something is a hold, not a tap, so a relic is
   seconds of standing still; the exit you came in by is not the exit you
   leave by.
4. **You come home and a light comes on.** The first room opens, paid for by
   what you carried. Your name goes on the roll for it.

**The session's job:** the hall is visibly different than when you arrived, and
you did that. Not a stat went up — a door opened.

## First week — about ?5–10 hours

**The hall goes from one lit room to most of the arrival floor**, and the mall
stops being a place you survive and becomes a place you know.

What changes in the player:

- **Routes become decisions.** The dock is reliable and pays less; the long way
  pays more and closes earlier. Learning the building is the skill.
- **The three-way choice arrives**, and it is the middle game's whole engine:
  the crate for the hall, the relic for the collection, or the supplies for
  your own kit. One trip cannot carry all three. (Rewards: `docs/REWARDS.md`.)
- **The meter becomes a clock.** Storm Charge has to make a lit crew's run
  measurably shorter than a dark crew's, or the lantern is free and the one
  line of the pitch is not true. Chris's number, still unset, and the most
  consequential one in the project.
- **The first Warden**, and the first wipe. You learn the loss rule by paying
  it once.
- **The workshop opens.** The relic-burden line is the first thing on the
  shelf, which pays off carrying relics rather than replacing the risk.

**The week's job:** you stop asking what the buttons do and start arguing with
your crew about whether to push.

## After ?30 hours — restored, not finished

**The hall is rebuilt and the landmarks are lit. The mall does not end.** It
changes register: from a place you are learning to a place you are testing
yourself against.

- **Conditions rotate.** `RaidDirector` already applies one wrinkle per raid —
  a blacked-out wing, a shutter open that should not be, a beacon pulling
  everything toward it. This is the seam the endgame plugs into: more
  conditions, rarer, sometimes two at once.
- **Deeper interiors.** The department store backs, the service corridors —
  the parts of the building a new keeper never had a reason to reach.
- **Variants, not bigger numbers.** A Window Walker that does not freeze. A
  Sweeper crew that patrols as one. Derived from the same jobs, per `ZONES.md`.
- **Collections and mysteries.** Relic sets that only mean something complete,
  and the archive gap the premise is explicit can never be filled — the oldest
  things in the Keeping Room are the ones nobody can name. That is a long-term
  pull that costs no new systems, only writing.
- **Other people's halls.** Visiting a friend's Lanternhold and raiding from it
  is already how the town code works. At thirty hours, helping someone else get
  their first light on is the thing you have that they do not.

**The endgame's job:** mastery, discovery, and being useful to your friends.
Never a number that goes up forever.

## What this page refuses

- **No second zone until this spine is fun in one.** `ZONES.md` already gates
  the solar farm behind the playtest, and that gate stands.
- **No prestige reset, no seasons, no battle pass.** The hall is the record of
  what you did; wiping it to sell the climb again is the one move that would
  break the premise.
- **Nothing sold that removes risk.** Locked in `DECISIONS.md`: not revives,
  not sprint, not enemy durability, not the lantern.
