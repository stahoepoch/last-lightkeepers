Status: **proposed scaffold**, 2026-11-01. Not a milestone, not a schedule, and
not a change to a locked rule. `docs/PRODUCTION-CONTROL.md` still holds: this
does not enter the active path until a played night earns expansion.

Read first, and do not rebuild what they already decided:

This page is the missing join. A quest list, a story, and a progression are
the same object here: a piece of a building, brought back, that lets the next
piece be reached, and wakes the thing whose job that piece was.

## The reason, said once

The valley has drowned before. The order kept its light and its records. The
old hall downtown held because the walls held, and strangers lived through a
night inside it. Later the order moved out past the farms into a building too
big for them, and they have been finishing it a room at a time ever since.
That building is Lanternhold.

This storm did not invent a villain. It woke every system that never received
a shutdown order. A department store is still trying to prevent loss. A
hospital is still trying to isolate. A library is still trying to stop a
record from leaving. The machines are how those buildings keep working. They
contain, clear, restock, lock, and wait for a supervisor who is not coming.

A Lightkeeper is dangerous to those systems for one reason: an unrecognized
person, inside, taking things out and turning power back on. The crew is not
saving the world from monsters. The crew is doing the order's old work at a
larger scale. Keep a light. Keep a record. Make one more building hold
through one more week.

That is why there are quests. A job exists because a room at home is dark,
or a route is shut, or a person is filed somewhere a building will not open.
The salvage is whatever that room was waiting on. The threat is the same
system, pleased to have its power back.

Nothing in the story needs to be told. The player meets it as a closed door,
a tide line, a ledger line with a blank for a name, and a machine that was
standing still until the circuit closed.

## What a job is allowed to be

`docs/QUESTS.md` already has the four rules. They stay, and one joins them:

5. **The job answers a question the previous job made askable.** If nobody
   would wonder it yet, the job is early. If the wonder can be satisfied by
   counting objects, the job is not a job.

A job is still five things and no more. A line in the ledger. A place that
is visibly not working. A cost in Supplies, Components, or crates, never
relics. A thing that opens, which you can see from where you stand. A thing
that wakes, stated before you accept, and permanent. No quest giver, no
marker that is not the building itself, no state machine. The next job
becomes real by gaining a `restoration` block like `DepartureGenerator`.

Nothing on the ladder makes light free, lowers Storm Charge, or weakens an
enemy. A pump buys time in the water. It does not delete the basement.

## Boswell's is act one

The game begins in one sealed anchor, **Boswell's**, the oldest of the four
named in `DECISIONS.md` row 19. Sayers, Penhurst, and Bellevue stay shut
behind the concourse. They are not four copies of this act.

Boswell's is the right first building because the hall is unfinished and a
department store is full of the things that finish buildings. A crate is
stamped with the department it came from. A room at home opens on crates
from the department that would have supplied it. Tonight's floor is whichever
floor the dark room at home is asking for. That is the middle of the game,
and it is already specified in `docs/THE-STORE.md`. This page only assigns
the ask.

The floors already teach in an order. The jobs follow that order so the
story and the syllabus are the same walk.

| Home, still dark | Asks for | Floor in Boswell's | Why that floor has it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generator room | Fuses and wiring | Basement plant | The store kept its own lights ready |
| Workshop | The maintenance cage | Staff corridors off receiving | The tools are still hung where the crew left them |
| Bedding, later the dormitory | Bulk cloth | Second, menswear and linens | The intact floor, where the stock was never touched |
| Lift house | Motors and rails | Stockrooms | Heavy things live where the public never walked |
| Kitchen | Housewares, a stove | Third | The hall eats what the lunch counter and the galleries kept |
| Radio room | A set, and the parts to wake it | Deeper offices | Credit and accounts still has the electronics |
| Meeting room | Chairs, and the programmes | Fourth, the auditorium | One way in. The town already used this store as a hall |

Relics are not on that table. They are names. They go to the Keeping Room
and stay there. They never pay for a door.

### The five jobs, as reasons

These are Chris's five from `docs/QUESTS.md`. The trades do not move. What
follows is why a person would do each one, and what the crew is allowed to
understand afterwards. Understanding means they can say it from what they
saw. It does not mean a line of dialogue played.

**1. Restore the departure generator.** The board in the side room winds for
twelve seconds and sometimes sends everyone back, because the generator
behind it is dead. Boswell's basement still has the fuses, in the plant that
used to bring the sales floor up every morning. Bring them home and the
board becomes honest. The cost is written on the ledger before anyone goes:
powered lights in the store, and the Window Walkers no longer need a keeper's
lantern to stand up. What the crew now knows is small and enough. The store
is not abandoned. It is closed for the night, and the night did not end.

**2. Recover the workshop tools.** The bench at home is under a sheet. The
cage is in the staff run off receiving, which is shut. Opening that run is
how a noisemaker gets earned instead of handed out on minute one. Sweepers
patrol it afterwards because that corridor was their route and the route has
power again. What the crew now knows: "staff only" is still in force. The
building sorts people. The crew does not sort as staff.

**3. Bring back the lift parts.** The shaft at home is gated, and the upper
floor of Boswell's is gated the same way. The parts are in the stockrooms,
heavy, and a hold in a place with one exit. Home gets its second floor. The
store gets its second floor too, and that floor is the intact one: folded
stock, displays, mannequins that have their department back. What the crew
now knows, and this is the turn of act one: the ruined floors were the kind
ones. The floor nothing happened to is the floor that still has its staff.

**4. Restore the Lanternhold radio.** The desk in the radio room has a lead
and no set. The set, and the boards around it, are deeper in, past the
credit office, which is the first room in the building that is about names
rather than goods. Home hears a carrier. The store hears the carrier too.
Glimmers wake, because reporting a fault was their job, and the crew has
become a fault the building can locate. The ledger already says the restored
line: somebody, somewhere, is still transmitting.

Proposed reading of that sentence, and it is a call: the voice is not a
person yet. It is the store and whatever is left of the old downtown plant
handshaking, degraded instructions, no quest giver hiding in the static. A
living voice would become an NPC with a shopping list, which this ladder
refused on purpose. The first human voice belongs to a contained keeper,
later, who can tell the crew what one of those instructions means. Until
then the radio gives places, not orders. A lit bay. A shutter that should
be down. A platform that is still being routed.

**5. Open the loading dock gate.** The recovery crew can reach the bay and
cannot reach the mall. The gate is seized, and it takes more than one night,
which is why it feels like a gate and not a key. Nothing at home opens. The
reward is the concourse, and the Warden — loss prevention, which wants the
crew to leave with nothing — now has a straight road to the bay the crew
just made usable. What the crew now knows: Boswell's was one door. The hill
is a whole building.

Rungs two through four are written here in that order because the floors
teach in that order. `docs/QUESTS.md` leaves open whether a crew may do them
in another order and come home with a different building. That is still the
more interesting version, and the worse one to teach with. Proposed: the
first time through, the ledger offers them in this order. A second profile
may pick. Chris's call, unchanged.

### The four jobs still inside the store

Proposed in `docs/QUESTS.md`, now placed.

**The pump.** The bargain basement has the best Components and the night
takes that floor first. The pump is in the plant. Restoring it holds the
water back during a run. It does not drain the building. Lesson five on the
ground-floor curriculum is a black floor with no choice about light, and a
dry basement would delete it. The crew buys twenty more minutes where the
motors are. They do not buy a new map.

**The well.** The escalator fall is choked. Clearing it is a fast way
between floors, for the crew and for anything that uses a route. Stated on
the ledger as such. After this, "go up" stops meaning "find the stairs the
collapse left."

**The bell.** The public-address panel is how the store called its staff.
Restoring it gives the crew a noise they can aim, somewhere else, on
purpose. The staff still answer. They answer at the panel too, so the decoy
and the danger are the same object. This is the first tool that is a
decision rather than a key.

**The auditorium.** Chairs and programmes, four floors up, one way in, under
a roof the storm is already using as a light. The programmes are the story
beat act one has been walking toward. The town held its meetings in this
store. The order's work and Boswell's work were the same kind of work: a
room, a roll of names, a light over a gathering. The meeting room at home
opens. The full roll wall opens with it. No one explains this. The
programme is in the crate, and the wall at home gains names that were
already in the building.

The Ficus needs no rung. It is a houseplant until the lantern is the only
sun in the room. It belongs to the fourth floor and the planters, and it
arrives when the crew is up there for the programmes.

## What progression is

Four records. They do not convert into each other.

| Record | What it is | Lost how | Pays for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The haul** | What is in your hands tonight | A wipe, or the night closing | Nothing, until it is home |
| **The kit** | Supplies and Components | Spent, on purpose | The workshop line, and the salvage half of a rung. Never the lantern, never speed, never a revive |
| **The roll** | Crates, credited by name, never removed | It cannot be lost | Rooms at home. Landmarks, which are spectacle. The gross count and the rooms are different lines |
| **The record** | A document, a programme, a keeper's account | It is not a stack | One new thing the hall can do. No counter, no shop |

`docs/REWARDS.md` already forbids a fifth currency. Knowledge is the thing
most likely to become one by accident, so it is capability: you recover a
document and a pump, a route, or a flood work becomes possible. You do not
spend the document.

People are not a record. A contained keeper comes home, stands in a hall
that is too big, and tells one thing they saw deeper in. They are not a
tally and they are not a lantern. Humans keep every lantern. A rescue is
infrastructure when the crew would rather open a route than walk someone
down four floors. `docs/CONTAINED-KEEPERS.md` is the contract. The mall's
version is the loss-prevention office behind the shutters, and it waits
until the dock gate exists, because that office is on the far side of it.

The workshop stays the small shelf it already is. The relic harness and the
padded cradle reduce a carry penalty and never erase it. Nothing else goes
on that shelf until a played night says the burden is the wrong argument.
Interaction speed stays off the shelf. Selling it means inventing a
restriction so the shop can remove it.

A crew's building is their fingerprint: which rooms are lit, which rungs are
done, whose name is on the line. Visiting a friend's hall is the long
progression. Helping them get a first light on is the thirty-hour game
`docs/SPINE.md` already names. There is no prestige reset. The hall is the
save file.

## After the gate, the valley is the rest of the story

The short version is below. The long version — who is still running the
valley, why a keeper was sent, and what the airport, the rink, the incline,
the foundry, the hospital, the stone bridge and the downtown are for — is
`docs/THE-VALLEY.md`. Nothing in either page is a build.

Zone one is Boswell's, then the concourse it earns. Nothing below is a build.
`docs/ZONES.md` locks the scope and the order: each place is more dangerous
in a way a player can name, and each one inverts the problem before it. Each
one is also a single civic job, in the same five-part shape. One sentence on
the ledger. One system. One violation of that place's last job.

**The concourse.** The mall thinks it is still preventing loss. The job is
the night gates and the directory, not a second Boswell's. Opening them
makes Sayers, Penhurst, and Bellevue into doors rather than four full
campaigns. Sayers is hardware, and it is where the bell's remaining parts
are if Boswell's panel is only half a tool. Penhurst is furnishings, a
second source for the meeting room, skippable if the auditorium already
dressed it. Bellevue faces the road down toward the valley, and its door is
how the next place gets onto the horizon. The wake is mall-scale lockdown:
shutters that used to be one store's problem become the whole hill's.

**The solar farm.** The first place whose job was to keep people out. Light
is no longer scarce, so the job cannot be "bring power home" without turning
the lantern into a souvenir. The job is a dark path through the array, and
the charge logs that say which downtown systems are still being fed. The
wake is the grid noticing a load. The contained version of a person here is
stranded outside a fence that will not open. There is no one to escort
through the light. You open a gap, or you do not.

**The rail yard.** The hills give out. The station is still routing an
evacuation to platforms that are not there. The job is one switch held long
enough that the recovery rig has a way down that is not the drowned road.
The wake is the trains and the gates moving again, because a route is a
route. A contained keeper is behind a platform gate the system will not
release while it still believes the platform is unsafe. Override the gate,
or restore a walk the system will accept as clear. Both are the contract.

**The works.** The mill on the valley floor, vertical, both kinds of light
at different heights. This is where a basement pump stops being a personal
reprieve and becomes a map of the city's water. The job is the mill's own
log of which downtown pumps still answer. The wake is those pumps answering.
The crew does not stop the flood. They learn which night, later, they can
hold one district above the water for a few hours.

**The hospital, a wing off that road.** Not a zone. An emergency-care system
with no one left to tell it to stop. The job is one isolation room. The
building thinks it is protecting a patient by never opening the door. The
crew overrides the seal or restores a corridor the protocol will treat as
clean. The person inside has seen the library locked as contamination. That
is the lead. It is one sentence, spoken once, at home, after they are safe.
It is not a briefing.

**The city.** The Point, the bridge, the old hall. Relighting the hall is
the order walking back into the building that founded it. It is a step. The
walls held once without anyone saving them. Putting a light back in that
room is the crew proving they understood the founding fact. The storm is
still coming down the valley while they do it.

**The library and the museum.** The only place on the ladder whose entire
job is to refuse the crew's entire job. Records do not leave. Objects go
back to storage. People read as contamination. The last job is to take one
body of knowledge out anyway: how this valley has held water before. That
document does not open a shop. It lets Lanternhold do a flood work the hall
could not do at the start. Civilization, in the only sense this game uses
the word, is a week the town can live. The ending is that the week can be
built. It is not that the weather stopped.

The burned gap in the Keeping Room stays empty. The earliest records are
gone to a fire from before any flood, and no run fills them. The oldest
objects on the shelves remain the ones nobody can name. That is the long
pull that must not be completed.

## How a player would tell it

If the scaffold is right, a player can say these sentences at these times,
without having been told them.

- After the first night: the hall is different, and I did that.
- After the generator: the store is still trying to open.
- After the second floor: the intact rooms are the bad ones.
- After the radio: something downhill is still answering.
- After the gate: the store was the front door.
- After the auditorium: this town has done this before, in rooms like ours.
- After the library: we did not stop the storm. We learned how to hold.

Any beat that needs a cutscene to become true is the wrong beat. Four people
on voice will talk over it. The building has to be the speaker.

## What this page will not do

- It will not put a second zone in the current milestone.
- It will not add a quest system beyond one block per job.
- It will not name the real valley, or a real order, or give this order a
  name. That last one is still open in `docs/PREMISE.md`, and a name invented
  here would be the wrong place to invent it.
- It will not fill the archive, sell a revive, or make the lantern safe.
- It will not turn Sayers, Penhurst, and Bellevue into three more campaigns.
  They are doors on a concourse until a played Boswell's says the game wants
  more building.
