# How the player learns any of this

Status: **proposed**, 2026-10-21. Chris asked the question that needed asking:
*"how does someone playing the game actually know any of this? It's not like
the Lanternhold lobby actually does anything to tell you."*

He is right. Every word of the setting currently lives in `docs/`, which is
nowhere a player will ever be. The hub has geometry and tags and says nothing.

**Ratified and sharpened, 2026-10-27.** Chris: *"The department store is the
tutorial."* `DECISIONS.md` row 43, LOCKED. This page argued that the setting
has to arrive by arrangement rather than by text; row 43 makes that binding and
names the instrument. There is no tutorial level, because the first real level
is one. The floor-by-floor curriculum is in `docs/THE-STORE.md`; this page
stays the doctrine behind it.

One thing the platform change costs this page: **the third channel got
quieter.** "Other players explaining it to each other" assumed Roblox's
constant supply of strangers. Row 36 is 1-4 private co-op on Steam, so a lobby
is three friends who bought the game the same day and a solo player who has
nobody. Channel three is now a bonus, not a mechanism, and anything that was
leaning on it has to be carried by channels one and two.

## The rule

**Nobody reads a codex.** Not on Roblox, not anywhere. A wall of text in a lobby
is read by about one player in fifty, and they are already the ones who would
have looked it up.

So the setting has to arrive three ways, in this order of power:

1. **Arrangement** — what the player sees, with no words at all.
2. **One line, attached to a thing they earned.**
3. **Other players explaining it to each other.**

Anything that needs a paragraph is a thing the game has failed to show.

## 1. Arrangement: the first sixty seconds

A new player spawns at the Landing. Before anyone types anything, they should
be able to work out four things by looking:

| What they see | What it tells them |
| --- | --- |
| A hall far too big for the people in it | There used to be more of us |
| Most of it dark, rooms visible but shut | There is a lot left to put right |
| A wall of lanterns, some lit | Other people are out there right now |
| One warm room with long tables, in use | We are getting by, and we are not alone |

That is the entire premise, delivered in four seconds, with no text. It is also
almost free — it is the arrangement of rooms we are already building.

**The dark is the storytelling.** A room you cannot enter says more than a sign
explaining what it will be.

## 2. One line, attached to something earned

The only text a player reliably reads is text about a thing they personally
just did.

**Relics are the engine.** They are the lantern-only tier, so they cost
something to get. Bring one home, it goes on a plinth in the Keeping Room, and
it gets **one or two sentences** — what it was, and whose it was.

That is the whole lore delivery system. Thirty relics is thirty fragments, each
one read willingly because the player earned it, and the story assembles over
weeks across a whole server rather than being handed over at the door.

It also means **the risky choice is the only way to learn anything.** A player
who never lights their lantern finishes runs and knows nothing. That is the
game teaching its own thesis.

Rules for relic text:
- Two sentences. Never three.
- About a person, not about the world. "Marguerite kept this in her coat for
  thirty years" beats "the storm struck in the fourth year".
- Never explain the mechanics. The plaque is not a tutorial.
- The oldest ones say *unknown*. The burnt case is the point: some things are
  gone and nothing brings them back.

**Four or five plaques on the building itself**, one line each, carry the rest:
by the door, by the Lamp Wall, by the empty case. One of them says the hall
held when the water came. That is the founding fact and it is one sentence.

## 3. Other players

The strongest channel, and the one nobody designs for.

A new player walks into a Keeping Room with sixty named relics in it and asks
*"what is all this?"* — and a player who has been here a month tells them.
Secondhand, wrong in places, far more memorable than any sign.

Design **for the question being asked**, not for the answer being available:

- Make progress visible, so there is something to point at.
- Put names on it — the Good Cheer Club roll, the credited landmarks — so the
  answer is partly *"that was me"*.
- Let the Lamp Wall show people who are out right now, so the hub is somewhere
  players stand around in rather than pass through.

## What already does this work

Worth noticing how much is free:

- **The enemies explain themselves.** A Sweeper in a cleaner's housing, a
  Window Walker that is visibly a shop mannequin, a Ficus that is a potted
  plant. Nobody needs telling these were the staff. Keep the silhouettes
  readable and that lore delivers itself.
- **The HUD already says it.** *"Dark — storm still rising."* Four words, and
  it tells you the meter does not care what you do.
- **The lantern teaches the premise by hurting you.** Light it, watch the meter
  climb faster, and you have learned that light feeds them without being told.

## What this needs that does not exist yet

Nothing here is built. In rough order of value for effort:

1. Relic plaque text, and the plinth showing it. The plinths exist and are
   tagged; nothing writes on them.
2. Four or five one-line plaques in the hall.
3. The Keeping Room's empty burnt case labelled as such.
4. The Lamp Wall actually reflecting live crews — the fixtures exist, the
   wiring does not.
5. Names on the Good Cheer Club roll.

None of it is scope creep: items 1, 3, 4 and 5 are already in
`docs/LANTERNHOLD.md` as v1 rooms, unwired.

## Open questions

- Who writes relic text — us, or players submitting it? Player-written is
  enormously cheaper and enormously riskier, and on Roblox it needs
  moderation.
- Does a relic's line appear the moment you hand it in, or next time you visit?
  Immediate is a reward; delayed makes people come back.
- How many relics before the story is legible? Thirty is a guess, not a number.
