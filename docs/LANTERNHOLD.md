# Lanternhold — the hub

Status: **proposed**, 2026-10-20. Read `docs/PREMISE.md` first; this assumes it.

Name decided by Chris: **Lanternhold**, not Light Haven.

## What it is

Where you are before you go up, and where you come back to. Cut into the valley
wall — old mine entries, above the waterline, below the heights. Warm, lit,
and mostly unfinished.

The handoff calls it "the warm underground town of Lanternhold". Underground
and uphill at once: it is inside the hill, and the hill is above the flood.

## The thesis: this town is good at this

The valley has drowned before. More than once. The storm is the latest time,
not the first.

That one fact gives the handoff's required tone — *hopeful and warm at home* —
without making it naive. Lanternhold is not a desperate bunker full of
frightened people. It is a community that has done this before and knows the
drill, which is exactly why there is a kitchen before there is an armoury.

**Not hope. Practice.** Everything built here should read as competent and
unhurried.

## The spaces

### The Incline Head — departure and return

Runs start and end here. The car comes down and the first thing you see is the
town, lit, from inside it.

This is also where you wake after a wipe, which the handoff already specifies
("it wakes in Lanternhold with half of unbanked loot"). Make arrival the
payoff — the emotional beat of the whole loop lands in this room.

### The Lamp Wall — the town is inhabited

A wall of lanterns, one per Lightkeeper currently out on a run.

- While you are up there, **your lamp burns here**, where everyone can see it.
- Extract, and it is taken down and carried home.
- Wipe, and **your lamp goes out in front of the whole town.**

No text, no kill feed, no combat. Other people's runs become legible at a
glance, the hub feels inhabited even when you are standing still, and a player
logging in sees lamps burning and wants to know whose they are.

Cheap to build, and it is the single strongest "this place is real" device in
the hub.

### The Keeping Room — the order's vault

Where relics go. Bring one back and it is **named**: shelved with a plaque
saying what it was and who it belonged to.

This is the lore engine. Relics are the lantern-only loot tier, so the only way
the story arrives is by someone taking the dangerous choice. A player who plays
it safe finishes runs and learns nothing. A player who burns light hears the
town speak. Nobody has to read a codex, and "I want to see more" is earned
rather than written.

The room fills over the life of the server. A new player walks into a hundred
named objects and wants to know what happened here.

Per `docs/PREMISE.md` this is the order's vault of records, not a trophy shelf —
which is a far better reason for it to exist.

### Your workshop corner — personal and persistent

Per-player, persistent, visible to visitors. Lenses, rescue harnesses,
water-resistant packs, decoration. Effort is never erased.

This is also where monetisation legitimately lives, because the handoff says
sell belonging, identity and decorating — never success in a run.

### The Community Kitchen — the first shared landmark

v1's server-wide landmark. Long tables, church-basement energy: the thing towns
actually do after a flood. Post-run buffs and food cosmetics.

Contributions credited by name. No player can undo or monopolise the work.

### Later landmarks (handoff, future)

- **Signal Tower** — clearer map and storm forecasts.
- **Garden Atrium** — greenery underground, growing under lamps. The strongest
  "the town is alive again" signal in the game.
- **Transit Lift** — unlocks the next zone. This is the Incline, extended.

## The principle: build most of it dark

Not missing. **Visibly there and unlit.** Doors that do not open. A tunnel with
no lamps in it. A room you can see into but not enter. Rebuilding lights them,
one at a time.

A hub that is twenty percent lit and eighty percent obviously-a-place tells the
whole story of the game without a line of dialogue. It answers "why am I going
back up there" every time the player turns around.

It is also the cheapest possible first build: it needs very little *finished*
content and a lot of *visibly unfinished* content.

## What v1 needs

The handoff's v1 scope already lists "Lanternhold hub with one personal
workshop corner" and the Community Kitchen. Both are unbuilt. This is not new
scope — it is the half of version one that makes the other half mean something,
because pillar one is "loot has a destination" and right now loot has none.

Minimum for v1:

1. Incline Head you depart from and return to.
2. One personal workshop corner.
3. The Community Kitchen, contributable, credited by name.
4. Enough visibly dark town to make the point.

The Lamp Wall and the Keeping Room are the two highest-value additions beyond
that, and the Keeping Room is what makes relics worth the risk.

## Deliberately not in v1

Signal Tower, Garden Atrium, Transit Lift, a second zone, any NPC dialogue
system. The handoff is explicit: do not expand scope before the version-one
loop passes its playtest question — do testers kill their lanterns, bank their
haul, and argue for one more room?

## Open questions

- Does the hub need NPCs at all, or do the Lamp Wall and the Keeping Room carry
  the social weight on their own? Cheaper and arguably stronger without.
- Who names a relic — an NPC archivist, or the player who brought it in?
  Player-authored text is enormously cheaper and enormously riskier.
- How much of the dark town is visible from the Incline Head on your first
  arrival? That shot is the game's first impression.
