# Lanternhold — the hub

Status: **proposed**, 2026-10-20. Read `docs/PREMISE.md` first; this assumes it.

Name decided by Chris: **Lanternhold**, not Light Haven.

## What it is

**The order's hall, out past the farms.** Not a bunker, not a cave — a building
too large for the people in it, which they moved into before it was finished
and have been finishing ever since.

Where you are before you go in toward the city, and where you come back to.

An earlier draft had this underground in mine workings. Chris corrected it: the
seams there are small and nobody has an underground base. Rural is better
anyway — it makes the whole progression legible without a word of explanation.
**You are safe because you are far.** Everything you want is closer in.

## The thesis: this town is good at this

The valley has drowned before. More than once. The storm is the latest time,
not the first.

That gives the handoff's required tone — *hopeful and warm at home* — without
making it naive. Lanternhold is not a frightened place. It is a community that
has done this before and knows the drill, which is exactly why there is a
kitchen before there is an armoury.

**Not hope. Practice.** Everything built here should read as competent and
unhurried.

## The hall was never finished, and that is the point

The real building this is taken from laid its cornerstone in 1931 and did not
finish its main room until 1948. The order moved in with one of two halls done,
got caught by the Depression, met in a side room for the better part of two
decades, and completed the place a room at a time as the money came in.

So: **build most of Lanternhold dark.**

Not missing. **Visibly there and unlit.** Doors that do not open. A corridor
with no lamps in it. A hall you can see into from the landing but not enter.
Rebuilding lights them, one at a time.

A hub that is twenty percent lit and eighty percent obviously-a-place tells the
whole story of the game without a line of dialogue. It answers "why am I going
back in there" every time the player turns around. It is also the cheapest
possible first build — very little *finished* content, a lot of *visibly
unfinished* content.

And it is true. This is what the order actually did.

## The Good Cheer Club

Kept, by Chris's call, from the real history: the members who raised the money
to finish the building formed themselves into a Good Cheer Club.

**This is the shared-contribution system.** Not a menu labelled "donate" — a
club with a ledger, a board, and names on it. When a crew hauls restoration
crates home, they are contributing to the Good Cheer Club, and the Club is what
lights the next room.

It does three jobs at once:

- It names the handoff's requirement that landmark contributions are *credited
  by name and cannot be undone or monopolised*. A club roll does exactly that.
- It makes the shared progression warm rather than administrative. You are not
  filling a progress bar; you are joining the people who pay for the lights.
- It is period-true and slightly funny, which is the tone the handoff asks for.

The Community Kitchen is the Club's first project.

## The spaces

### The Landing — departure and return

Where the road out meets the hall. Runs start and end here, and the first thing
you see coming home is the building, lit, with the dark parts obvious behind
the lit ones.

This is also where you wake after a wipe, which the handoff already specifies
("it wakes in Lanternhold with half of unbanked loot"). Make arrival the
payoff — the emotional beat of the whole loop lands here.

### The Lamp Wall — the hall is inhabited

A wall of lanterns, one per Lightkeeper currently out.

- While you are out there, **your lamp burns here**, where everyone can see it.
- Extract, and it is taken down and carried home.
- Wipe, and **your lamp goes out in front of the whole hall.**

No text, no kill feed, no combat. Other people's runs become legible at a
glance, the hub feels inhabited while you are standing still, and a player
logging in sees lamps burning and wants to know whose they are.

Cheap to build, and the strongest "this place is real" device in the hub.

### The Keeping Room — the order's vault

Where relics go. Bring one back and it is **named**: shelved with a plaque
saying what it was and who it belonged to.

This is the lore engine. Relics are the lantern-only tier, so the only way the
story arrives is by someone taking the dangerous choice. A player who plays it
safe finishes runs and learns nothing; a player who burns light hears the hall
speak. Nobody reads a codex, and "I want to see more" is earned rather than
written.

**The gap is from fire, not water.** The order's earliest records burned long
before any flood. Nothing brought back from a run will ever fill that shelf —
the oldest things in the room are the ones nobody can name, and they stay that
way. Worth building the empty case on purpose.

### Your workshop corner — personal and persistent

Per-player, persistent, visible to visitors. Lenses, rescue harnesses,
water-resistant packs, decoration. Effort is never erased.

Also where monetisation legitimately lives: the handoff says sell belonging,
identity and decorating — never success in a run.

### The Community Kitchen — the Club's first project

v1's shared landmark. Long tables, church-basement energy: the thing a hall
actually does after a flood. Post-run buffs and food cosmetics.

Contributions credited on the Club roll. No player can undo or monopolise it.

### Later rooms (handoff, future)

- **Signal Tower** — clearer map and storm forecasts. Needs power, which is why
  the solar farm is zone two.
- **Garden Atrium** — growing things under lamps. The strongest "we are alive
  again" signal in the game.
- **Transit Lift** — unlocks the next zone. The Incline, extended.

## What v1 needs

The handoff's v1 scope already lists "Lanternhold hub with one personal
workshop corner" and the Community Kitchen. Both are unbuilt. This is not new
scope — it is the half of version one that makes the other half mean something,
because pillar one is "loot has a destination" and right now loot has none.

Minimum:

1. The Landing, to leave from and return to.
2. One personal workshop corner.
3. The Community Kitchen, contributable, credited on the Club roll.
4. Enough visibly dark hall to make the point.

The Lamp Wall and the Keeping Room are the highest-value additions beyond that,
and the Keeping Room is what makes relics worth the risk.

## Deliberately not in v1

Signal Tower, Garden Atrium, Transit Lift, any second zone, any NPC dialogue
system. The handoff is explicit: do not expand scope before the version-one
loop passes its playtest question — do testers kill their lanterns, bank their
haul, and argue for one more room?

## Open questions

- Does the hub need NPCs at all, or do the Lamp Wall, the Club roll and the
  Keeping Room carry the social weight on their own? Cheaper and arguably
  stronger without.
- Who names a relic — an archivist, or the player who brought it in?
  Player-authored text is enormously cheaper and enormously riskier.
- How much of the dark hall is visible from the Landing on a first arrival?
  That shot is the game's first impression.
