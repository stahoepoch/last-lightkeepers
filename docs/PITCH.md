# How to explain Last Lightkeepers


For saying out loud. Start with the one line; go further only if they lean in.

## The one line

**Your lantern is the only way to find the good stuff — and the thing that
gets you caught.**

## The eight-year-old version

> Imagine the power went out everywhere, and it never came back on.
>
> The robots that used to clean the mall and stack the shelves are still there,
> and they still work. But they run on light — that's how they charge up. In
> the daytime they're wide awake and there are way too many of them. At night
> they go quiet. Some just stand still, like statues.
>
> So you go at night.
>
> You take a lantern. If you turn it on, you can see the really good stuff —
> treasure hidden back in the dark. But the light wakes the robots up, and they
> come looking for you.
>
> That's the whole game. Your light is how you find the best things. Your light
> is also how they find you.
>
> You go alone, or with up to three friends. You grab what you can carry.
> Nothing you pick up is really yours until you get out of the building — so
> the whole game is one argument: we've got enough, call the ride now and hold
> them off... or risk one more store?
>
> If a robot gets you, you don't die — you fall down, and a friend can run over
> and pick you back up. If *everybody* falls down, you wake up back home and
> lose half of whatever you didn't stash.
>
> And home is the best part. Home is a huge old building, and most of it is
> dark. There are whole rooms you can see into but can't go in yet. Everything
> you bring back turns another light on. A kitchen. A staircase. A big hall.
> The more trips everyone makes, the more of it lights up — and your name goes
> on the wall for the parts you paid for.
>
> Some things you find are relics, from before the storm. You can only see
> those if your lantern is on. Bring one home and somebody tells you what it
> was and who it belonged to.
>
> So the only way to find out what happened here is to take the risk.

## The grown-up version, about a minute

Co-op scavenging game on Roblox, one to four players, no PvP and no griefing.

A storm took the valley and never really left. The machines that serviced the
place — cleaners, stockers, maintenance drones, mall security — are still
running, and they run on light. Daylight feeds them, so nobody works in it. At
night they drop to reserve, and that's your window.

You go up from Lanternhold, the old hall out past the farms where what's left
of the order lives, and you scavenge whatever the storm left behind. Runs are
short — six to twenty minutes door to door.

Your lantern is the whole decision. Off, you're hard to find, but you can only
take what's lying in plain sight. On, you can see the rare caches — and you're
feeding every machine in the building. There's a shared meter that only ever
rises, faster the more lanterns are lit, and when it tops out the Warden comes
for whoever's brightest. You can't kill the Warden. You leave, or you hide.

Nothing is secured until you extract. There's no stash point and no safety
net: if the crew goes down, or the way out closes first, everything you found
that run is gone. What there is instead is a decision about *when to be loud*.
Calling the exit sets off a flare and a siren, spikes the meter whatever it was
sitting at, and brings everything in the building toward one room — then you
hold that room until the ride arrives. So every run ends with the same
argument: we have enough, call it now and defend it, or risk one more store?

What you bring back rebuilds the hall. Not stats — *lights*. Rooms that were
dark open up, one at a time, paid for by name on the Good Cheer Club roll. And
the relics you can only find with your lantern lit are what tell you the story
of what happened here.

## If they ask

**"Is it a shooter?"**
No. You can't really fight back. You hide, you distract, you carry each other,
you run. The skill is deciding when to light up and when to leave.

**"So it's like those extraction games?"**
Same family, much kinder in the ways that matter and harsher in one. No PvP,
nobody can steal from you, no gear to lose, runs are short, and you get knocked
down rather than killed. The harsh part: get out or lose the haul. There's no
stash point, so the tension comes from your own greed and from the clock,
never from other players.

**"What happens when I lose?"**
You wake up at home and that run's haul is gone. What you already brought home
on earlier runs is untouched, your upgrades and your hall are permanent, and
nobody can take anything off you. You lose the trip, never the progress.

**"What's the point of the loot?"**
It turns the lights on at home. The town is mostly dark on purpose — you can
see what you haven't rebuilt yet. That's the hook: there's always another room.

**"Why does it have to be dark?"**
Because the machines run on light. In daylight they're all awake and there's no
way through. That's also why your lantern is dangerous — it's a small sun.

**"Is it scary?"**
Tense, not nasty. You don't die, you get knocked down and picked up. Everything
makes a sound before you see it.

## What not to say

Don't call it a horror game, don't compare it to ARC Raiders, and don't name a
real town or a real fraternal order — the setting borrows a valley's shape, not
its name. See `docs/PREMISE.md`.
