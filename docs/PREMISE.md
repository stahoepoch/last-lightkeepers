# Premise — why the dark, and who you are

Status: **proposed**, 2026-10-20. Agreed in conversation with Chris; not yet in
the handoff PDF. Nothing here changes a rule — see "What this does not change".

Read `docs/LANTERNHOLD.md` next for the hub this implies.

## The question

"Why do we only scavenge in the dark? Why not in the daytime?"

A premise that can't answer that has a hole in it. "The storm makes it dark" is
a reason the *player* can't see. It is not a reason the *crew won't go*.

## The answer: the machines run on light

They were service units in a city that had gone solar — floor cleaners, shelf
stackers, maintenance drones, mall security. When the storm took the grid, they
fell back on ambient light.

- **In daylight they are fed.** All of them awake, fast, everywhere. There are
  no gaps to move through, so nobody goes.
- **At night they run on reserve.** Patrols slow. Some units stand dormant
  exactly where they stopped.

So a lantern is not only a thing that reveals you. It is a small sun, and you
are feeding them.

## What this locks into place

It is worth being explicit about how much of the existing design this explains
rather than replaces:

| Existing thing | What the premise makes it mean |
| --- | --- |
| **Window Walkers** stand still until light catches them | No longer a quirk of one enemy — it is the rule, stated plainly by the newest member of the roster |
| **Storm Charge only ever rises** | It is not weather. It is the machine population waking up, and nothing puts them back to sleep |
| **Light accelerates Storm Charge** | You are charging them directly |
| **Amber lanterns vs electric-blue storm** | Their power against yours. The colour-blind readability rule now carries the premise |
| **Zone two is a solar farm** (handoff) | That is where they charge. The escalation was written before the reason existed |

The storm, then, is not just cover. It is the electrical event that keeps them
alive, and the reason there is no usable daylight left anyway.

## What this does not change

**No mechanic moves.** Storm Charge still only rises, darkness still slows the
climb and never reverses it, one lantern is still ~2.5x baseline, light still
reveals relics and attracts danger. There is no "light repels small enemies"
exception. This is fiction laid over rules that already pass their tests, and
if any of it ever requires a rule to bend, the rule wins and this document is
wrong.

## Who the Lightkeepers are

**An order, not a job.**

Not scavengers who happen to carry lamps — an inherited fraternity whose work,
since the first flood, has been keeping the records and keeping a light
burning. The hall that survived became the hospital; the people who kept the
vault kept going.

This explains several things that were previously just mechanics:

- Why contributions to landmarks are **credited by name and cannot be undone**
  — that is what an order's ledger is.
- Why the Keeping Room exists at all, and why it is a **vault** rather than a
  trophy shelf.
- Why the town is **calm**. It has an institution, and the institution has done
  this before.
- The title. A Last Lightkeeper is not the last person alive. It is the last of
  an order.

## The valley

Corrected 2026-10-20 after Chris pointed out the real geography; an earlier
version of this had the crew descending to scavenge, which was wrong.

- **Lanternhold is cut into the valley wall** — old mine entries, above the
  waterline, below the heights.
- **The Incline runs up** from it to the hilltops, which is what an incline is
  for: getting out of a valley in a hurry.
- **The places worth robbing are up there**, on the high ground. The Galleria
  sits on a hill.

So the crew climbs to scavenge and comes down to warmth. Home is downhill.

## Real-world inspiration, and the naming rule

The valley is modelled on Johnstown, Pennsylvania — three floods, a steel and
coal town, an inclined plane built after the first disaster, a mall up on a
hill, and a hall downtown that survived the water and became a hospital with
the records still vaulted inside it.

**Decided by Chris, 2026-10-20: do not name Johnstown, and do not name any real
fraternal order.** Keep the texture, invent the names. The real places and the
real dead stay out of the game; what we take is the shape of a town that is
good at rebuilding, because it has had to be.

This matters most around the Keeping Room. Objects getting their names back
sits very close to a real cemetery full of unidentified dead. That is powerful
when it is deliberate and ugly when it is careless.

## Open questions

- Is there any usable daylight at all, or has the stormwall ended it? A dim
  grey day the crew refuses to work in is more interesting than no day at all.
- Does the order have a name?
- Do the machines visibly charge — a unit standing in a shaft of blue getting
  faster — or is it only felt through the meter?
