# The valley — the long story

Status: **proposed**, 2026-11-01. A story scaffold, not a milestone. It does
not enter `docs/PRODUCTION-CONTROL.md`. It does not rename Johnstown, and it
does not put the real dead into the game. `docs/PREMISE.md` already took the
shape of that valley — three floods, a hilltop field, an incline, a bridge,
a works, a hall that held — and left the names behind.

`docs/THE-WORK.md` is act one: Boswell's, the ledger, the hall. This page is
why the acts after it are one story instead of a list of maps.

## What the other games actually do

Three of them were worth stealing from. None of them should be copied.

**ARC Raiders** (Embark, set in 2180) does not open with the truth about its
machines. The surface belongs to ARC. They fell out of the sky after a
collapse, were fought off for ten years, then came back better, and the
living went underground into Speranza. You are sent topside because the city
cannot make what it needs. Quests are favors for people keeping that city
alive — bad water traced to a dam, power traced to a missing engineer, a
spaceport whose substations still matter. Who built ARC, and why, is still
unanswered in the game's own telling. The powerful had already left on the
Exodus. The mystery is the product. A campaign cutscene is not.

**Horizon Zero Dawn** answers the same question and then refuses to stop.
The machines are not a devil. They are a terraforming system, GAIA, whose
subordinate jobs — air, water, building new machines, and a failsafe that
can end the world — came unhooked and each believed it was the whole mind.
Aloy is not a chosen warrior from a prophecy the player is told at the
start. She is what the system made when it needed its maker's key, and she
learns that in the buildings, one ruin at a time. The cruelty, when it
arrives, is human. Ted Faro built a swarm that would not take a shutdown
order. He later erased the archive that would have taught the new world
what it was. The machines got stranger because nobody was left who could
tell a subsystem it was only a subsystem.

**The scrap-animal games** split into two lessons. Ratchet is a mechanic
from a dead people. He lives by turning other people's wrecks into tools,
and the plot is him finding out what his people were for. Stray is a cat in
a city the robots never stopped running: companions, a control that has
rotted, a way out that the city itself does not want opened. If the memory
was Biomutant instead, the useful part is the same. You build yourself out
of the ruin, and the world has a wound with more than one honest side.

The steal, for this valley:

- Send the crew for a practical reason. Let the historical reason arrive
  late, in a building, not in a briefing.
- Keep the machines procedural. Let them get stranger because they are still
  being supplied, not because they wake up evil.
- Put the nefarious turn on people, and on a plan that was once an act of
  care.
- Leave one question open longer than is comfortable. Who is in control is
  a bad fact to spend in act one.
- Make salvage the way the truth is carried home. A part, a log, a programme.
  Not a codec conversation.

## The answer, in four beats

The player should be able to say these, in this order, and not earlier.

1. **Nobody is in control.** The store is still at work. That is all.
2. **Something is still giving orders, and the orders are old.** The airport
   beacon is calling. The incline is still running an evacuation. The
   calls are years out of date, and they are being obeyed.
3. **This is not a ruin running down. It is a ruin being maintained.** The
   foundry is making parts for the other buildings. The night is in
   production.
4. **We wrote the orders.** After an earlier flood, the order helped build
   a disaster schedule so the town would not die waiting for a person to
   reach a switch. The schedule's hour started when this storm hit. Nobody
   sent the stand-down, because the people who knew how had already left
   the valley. Lanternhold has been sending keepers back in ever since.
   You were sent because your name was the next line on the rota.

There is no robot king. Row 47 stays. The machines contain, clear, lock,
route, and wait. What gets nefarious is that the schedule is competent, it
is still being fed, and the people who stayed believe that turning it off
will drown the lower streets tonight. They may be right.

## Where you came from, why you were sent, where you are going

**You came from Lanternhold**, out past the farms. The order moved there
when they understood the schedule would not stand down. They have been
finishing a hall that is too big for them. You did not come from a vault,
and you are not a copy of anyone. Horizon's "I was made for this" is the
wrong costume. The ARC shape fits: you are who the hall can spare.

**You were sent to restore operations.** That is the true sentence on the
ledger, and it is incomplete. Restore, without a second verb, feeds the
schedule. Every fuse you seat and every gate you free is a subsystem
getting its job back. The second verb is **divert**. The ledger does not
print it until the foundry, because the crew would not believe it at the
mall, and they should not be asked to. Divert means aiming the same
machines at the living: hold this district above water for one night, open
this route for the recovery crew, unlock this shelter because the all-clear
is never coming. Not off. Aimed.

**You are going down**, toward the city, and then home with one document
from the library. The document is not a new currency. It is the stand-down
the order failed to send, rewritten so the schedule holds water for the
town instead of completing a disaster hour from years ago. The storm still
comes. The burned gap in the Keeping Room still cannot be filled. The
oldest objects stay unnamed. That absence is the proof the story is not a
puzzle with every piece in the box.

## Who else is there

Four kinds of people, and no faction war. PvP stays out.

- **The hall.** The recovery crew, the roll, the kitchen. They want things
  brought home. They do not know the whole schedule. Most of them were born
  after the move.
- **The filed.** Contained keepers. The buildings processed them and waited
  for a supervisor. Each one you bring out knows one true thing about a
  deeper place. The first human voice on the radio is one of them, later,
  translating a single instruction. Until then the radio is machines
  handshaking.
- **The staff.** People who never left the valley. The schedule recognizes
  them, because they never stopped badging in. They keep a district alive
  by obeying it. They will help you restore a pump and they will fight you,
  or simply lock you out, when you try to divert the foundry. They are not
  a villain faction. They are the argument. Sabotage, from where they
  stand, is how the lower streets drown.
- **The absent.** The people who left from the airport when the schedule's
  hour began. Money, skill, and the copy of the manual that was supposed
  to stay. They are logs and empty gates. They are not coming back. The
  beacon is still asking them to.

because it is the proof the power is current. The stone bridge is the city
bridge, given the job the geography already implies: a span that catches
what the water brings.

## What "stranger" is allowed to mean

Stranger is a change in category, not a rise in monster rank.

- The store processes you as inventory and debris.
- The field processes you as a flight that missed its window.
- The incline processes you as an evacuee, and will take you to the wrong
  end of the valley if you stand in the car.
- The rink processes you as a headcount. Leaving early is a protocol breach.
- The foundry processes you as material if you stand on the line. That is
  the first time the verb is not contain or route. It is melt and recast.
  Row 47 still holds if the line was built to remake stock, not people, and
  the line does not know the difference. The moment a writer gives it a
  preference for flesh, the thesis is gone.
- The hospital processes you as a patient. This one will feel like a villain
  if the art direction blinks. It has to look like medicine.
- The library processes you as contamination. You are the loss event.

Nefarious, if the word is going to be used at all, belongs to two human
facts and nowhere else. The order left the schedule running and then left
the valley. The staff will hurt you to keep it running. Both of them can
say, truthfully, that they did it so people would live.

## How a night carries the plot

ARC's lesson on structure: do not pause the raid for the story. A night
still has one job on the ledger, one cost, one thing that opens, one thing
that wakes. The plot is which job it is.

A story beat is one of these, and it comes home in the haul:

- A log that makes an earlier place mean something else. The charge log.
  The shipping log. The minutes.
- A filed keeper, who speaks one sentence at the hall and then is a person
  who lives there, not a quest marker.
- A route the recovery crew can now drive. The incline and the yard are
  how the truck eventually reaches the valley floor. Until then, extraction
  is still the dock on the hill.
- A divert flag, permanent, ugly, and visible. The beacon goes quiet. The
  rink doors are unlocked and the scoreboard reads the all-clear you faked.
  The foundry's line is pouring brackets for pumps instead of shutters.
  The player can walk back and see that they changed the valley's mind.

What a night must not carry: a twist that only works if everyone stops
talking, a new currency called "truth," a boss who explains the schedule,
or a second Boswell's with a different coat of paint.

## The staff, so they are not a late surprise

They should be heard before they are met. The radio, in act one, is
handshake and old instruction. One of those instructions has a human voice
on a loop, badging a shift change at the foundry that happened years ago
and is still the shift change. The crew thinks it is a recording. It is a
recording. The live staff do not get on an open channel, because the
schedule treats an unrecognized transmission as a fault, and the staff know
that better than the crew does.

You meet them in person at the incline's mid-station or the rink's count-in,
whichever is built first. They are helpful. They want the plane running.
They want the doors to stay shut until the all-clear, and they will tell
you why, with a flood you can see from the window. The fight, if it is a
fight, is at the foundry, and it should be possible to finish that night by
making a deal instead of a clearance: one line diverted, the rest left
alone, their district still pumped. A story with only one honest side is
smaller than the valley you described.

## What this refuses

- A secret intelligence that hates the crew. If a later draft wants one, it
  has to retire row 47 in `docs/DECISIONS.md` in Chris's hand, with the
  player behavior it improves. This page does not retire it.
- The real town's name, the real order, the real arena, the real plane, the
  real bridge's dead. The shape is the incline, the rink, the stone span,
  the field on the hill. The names stay invented, and they are not invented
  here.
- Filling the burned archive. The library gives the manual for the
  schedule. It does not give back the earliest records. Those were ash
  before any flood.
- Explaining beat 4 at the mall. If the old hall's minutes can be found in
  Boswell's credit office, the long story collapses into act one and the
  descent has nothing to reveal.

## Open, and Chris's

- The four-beat answer. Especially beat 4: the order built the schedule.
  That makes "build things that hold" into "we built the thing that is
  holding us wrong." It is the strongest version. It is also a change in
  the moral weight of the order, and it is his.
- Whether the foundry line is allowed to treat a body as stock by
  indifference. The line above says yes, indifference only. A preference
  is a different game.
- Whether the staff can be bargained with, or whether the foundry night is
  a break. Bargaining is proposed, because a valley with only enemies is
  ARC's surface and not this hall.
- Where the rink sits. Proposed: at the foot of the incline, where evacuees
  were counted. If it sits downtown instead, the shelter beat lands later
  and harder, and the incline arrives without its human payoff.
- The single flood work the library unlocks. The shape is a stand-down
  rewritten as a hold. The machine it actually changes is unset.
- Names. Still unset on purpose.
